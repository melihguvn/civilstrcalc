About CivilStrCalc

Free structural engineering calculators — open to everyone, no registration required.

What is CivilStrCalc?

CivilStrCalc is a free, browser-based toolkit for structural engineers, students, and educators working with reinforced concrete and structural steel. Every calculation runs entirely in your browser — no data is sent to any server, no account is needed, and there are no paywalls.

The calculators implement the full provisions of the most widely used structural design codes: ACI 318-25 (United States), Eurocode 2 EN 1992-1-1 (Europe), AISC 360-22 (steel, United States), IS 456:2000 (India), and TS 500:2000 (Turkey). Each result comes with a step-by-step calculation report that can be printed or saved as a PDF.

Calculators & Tools

Reinforced Concrete Design

RC Beam Design Flexural (Whitney stress block), shear (stirrups), and torsion design for rectangular and T-section beams. ACI 318-25, EC2, IS 456.
RC Column Design P-Mx-My interaction diagram for rectangular and circular columns under biaxial bending. Seismic confinement checks.
Slab Design One-way flexural design and punching shear for flat-plate systems. Interior, edge, and corner columns. Stud rail and radial ring options.
Development Length Straight bar, standard hook, and headed bar development lengths. Modification factors per ACI 318-25 §25.5. Lap splice classes.
Crack Width Service-load crack width per Eurocode 2 §7.3 (wk formula) and ACI 318 (z-parameter). Durability exposure class checks.
Spread Footing Design Bearing pressure, flexural reinforcement, one-way and two-way shear for isolated spread footings. ACI 318-25 and EC2.

Steel Structure Design AISC 360-22

Steel Flexural Design Moment capacity (φbMn) for W, S, HP, C, and MC sections including compact/non-compact classification, local buckling, and lateral-torsional buckling (LTB) per AISC 360-22 Chapter F.
Steel Axial (Column) Design Compression capacity (φcPn) for wide-flange, HSS, and angle sections. Effective length KL, slenderness ratio KL/r, critical stress Fcr, and section class checks.
Steel Beam-Column Design Combined axial + flexure interaction check per AISC 360-22 §H1 (H1-1a/H1-1b equations). Covers uniaxial and biaxial loading cases.
Lateral-Torsional Buckling (LTB) Detailed LTB check: unbraced length Lb vs. Lp/Lr limits, Cb factor, plastic/inelastic/elastic zones, and φbMn output per AISC 360-22 §F2.
Truss Design Roof truss analysis and member design (Warren, Pratt, Howe configurations). SI and US unit modes. SHS, RHS, L-angle, HEB chord and web sections.

Retaining Wall Design

Retaining Wall — ACI (SI) Sliding, overturning, bearing, and flexural design per ACI 318-25 in SI units.
Retaining Wall — ACI (US) Same design procedure in US customary units (kips, ft, psi).
Retaining Wall — Eurocode 2 Gravity retaining wall design per EN 1992-1-1 and EN 1997-1 (Geotechnical).
Retaining Wall — TSC / TBDY Seismic retaining wall design per TS 500 and TBDY 2018 §16.12, including kh=0.4·r·SDS dynamic increment.

Reference Tables & Guides

Steel Section Database Searchable database of IPE, HEA/B/M, W, HSS, ISMB, ISMC profiles per EN 10365, AISC, and IS 808. Custom section input.
Rebar Properties Bar sizes and mechanical properties for ASTM A615/A706, EN 10080 B400/B500, IS 1786 Fe415–Fe600, and TS 708 grades.
Concrete Properties Design properties (fck, fcd, Ecm, strain limits) for all standard strength classes per ACI 318-25, EC2, and IS 456.
Unit Converter Instant conversion between SI and US customary units for force, length, stress, moment, and section properties.
ACI 318-25 Design Guide Comprehensive reference guide covering ACI 318-25 provisions for beams, columns, slabs, walls, and detailing.
AISC 360-22 Design Guide Reference guide for LRFD steel design: flexure, shear, axial, combined loading, and connection design.

Who Is It For?

CivilStrCalc is designed for anyone working with structural concrete or steel:

  • Practising engineers who need quick, reliable preliminary calculations before moving to full analysis software.
  • Students studying structural engineering who want to verify hand calculations or explore how code provisions work in practice.
  • Educators looking for a free classroom tool that shows every calculation step transparently.
  • Checkers and reviewers who need an independent calculation to cross-check design outputs.

Design Philosophy

Every intermediate step is shown. We believe structural engineering software should be a transparent aid to engineering judgement — not a black box. If you cannot follow every step in the report, the result should not be trusted.

All calculations are client-side: your inputs never leave your device. The tools work offline once the page is loaded, and there is no tracking of individual calculation inputs.
