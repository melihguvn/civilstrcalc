1. The Eurocode Family

The Eurocodes are a set of harmonised European Standards (EN) for the structural design of buildings and civil engineering works. Developed by CEN (European Committee for Standardisation), they replaced national codes across EU member states and are widely adopted beyond Europe in Turkey, the Middle East, and East Asia.

How EN 1990 Governs All Others

EN 1990 sits above all other Eurocodes. It defines the framework for reliability, the design value concept, and all load combination rules. Every material-specific Eurocode (EC2–EC9) operates within the EN 1990 framework and cannot be used independently. This is fundamentally different from the US system, where ASCE 7 and ACI 318 are co-equal codes that cross-reference each other.

Second-generation Eurocodes (EN 1992:2023 / EN 1993:2022): CEN published updated editions of EC2 and EC3 in 2022–2023. These revisions clarify provisions, update material tables, and reduce the number of Nationally Determined Parameters. This guide covers the core principles applicable to both the 2004 and 2022/2023 editions.

2. Limit State Design Philosophy

Eurocode structural design is based on the limit state method defined in EN 1990. A structure must not exceed any relevant limit state during its design working life (typically 50 years for buildings, 100 years for bridges).

Ultimate Limit States (ULS)

ULS concern safety of people and the structure. Once exceeded, the structure may collapse or otherwise fail catastrophically.

  • STR: Internal failure of the structure — flexure, shear, axial, connection failures of structural members
  • GEO: Failure or excessive deformation of the ground — bearing capacity, slope stability, retaining wall failure
  • EQU: Loss of static equilibrium — overturning, sliding of the structure as a rigid body
  • FAT: Fatigue failure — cyclic loading exceeding endurance limits (bridges, crane girders, offshore)
  • UPL: Hydraulic uplift — buoyancy in structures below the water table

Serviceability Limit States (SLS)

SLS concern the functioning of the structure or structural members under normal use, comfort, and appearance.

  • Deflection: L/250 quasi-permanent; L/500 for partitions sensitive to movement
  • Crack width: wk ≤ 0.3 mm (XC, XD, XS exposure); wk ≤ 0.4 mm (X0, XC1)
  • Vibration: Natural frequency limits for pedestrian comfort (fn > 1 Hz / 3 Hz)
  • Stress limitation: σc ≤ 0.6 fck (quasi-permanent) to avoid non-linear creep

The Design Value Inequality

EN 1990 — Fundamental Design Inequality
SI Ed ≤ Rd
Where:
Ed = design value of the effect of actions (moment, shear, axial force, etc.)
Rd = design value of the corresponding resistance
Rd = Rk / γM where γM is the partial safety factor for the material
Ed = E{γG·Gk + γQ,1·Qk,1 + γQ,i·ψ0,i·Qk,i}

The Eurocode approach divides safety between actions (load factors γG, γQ) and resistances (material factors γM). This dual-factor system differs from the single-factor LRFD approach (φRn ≥ ΣγQ) used in US codes, but achieves the same reliability target.

3. National Annexes

Eurocodes are published by CEN but implemented by each member state through a National Annex (NA). The NA specifies Nationally Determined Parameters (NDPs) — values that are left open in the EN text for national choice.

What National Annexes Can Modify

  • Load factors: γG, γQ — some countries use 1.35/1.50 (recommended), others adjust
  • Combination factors ψ: ψ0, ψ1, ψ2 for imposed loads, snow, wind
  • Material partial factors: γC, γS, γM — recommended values may be reduced for quality-controlled production
  • Seismic hazard maps: agR values and ground type definitions (EC8)
  • Exposure class limits: minimum concrete classes per exposure
  • National choice clauses: alternative methods where EN allows choice (e.g. EN 1990 Eq. 6.10 vs 6.10a/6.10b)

A complete Eurocode structural design follows a consistent sequence. Each stage draws on one or more Eurocodes.

  • Action Determination (EN 1991): Characteristic values of permanent actions Gk, variable actions Qk, snow sk, and wind qp. Site-specific parameters from National Annex maps.
  • Load Combinations (EN 1990): Combine factored actions using STR/GEO combinations. Critical to choose the governing combination correctly (Eq. 6.10 or 6.10a/6.10b).
  • Structural Analysis: Determine bending moments, shear forces, and axial forces. EC2 allows plastic redistribution (up to 30%); EC3 permits elastic-plastic analysis for Class 1/2 cross-sections.
  • ULS Design: Check MEd ≤ MRd, VEd ≤ VRd, NEd ≤ NRd for each member. Material resistance factors γM are applied at this stage.
  • SLS Verification: Deflections (quasi-permanent combination), crack widths (frequent or quasi-permanent), stress limitation, vibration checks.

5. Eurocode vs US Standards

Engineers working across jurisdictions frequently need to translate between the two systems. The philosophies are equivalent but the notation, factors, and specific requirements differ significantly.

Key Conceptual Differences

  • Concrete strength notation: Both systems use cylinder strength. EC2's fck and ACI's f'c are numerically equivalent (both 28-day cylinder) — a C30/37 concrete has fck = 30 MPa ≈ 4,350 psi.
  • Rebar yield: European B500B has fyk = 500 MPa; US Grade 60 has fy = 420 MPa (60 ksi). Design values: fyd = 500/1.15 = 435 MPa (EC2) vs fy = 420 MPa (ACI, no material factor applied).
  • Safety format: EC2 applies γC = 1.5 to concrete and γS = 1.15 to rebar, then uses unfactored φ = 1.0. ACI applies φ = 0.65–0.90 to resistance (equivalent approach, different notation).
  • Wind and seismic: EC8 response spectrum uses PGA + soil factor S; ASCE 7 uses spectral accelerations SDS/SD1. Seismic hazard maps are country-specific for EC8.
