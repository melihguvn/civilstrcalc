1. The US Standards Ecosystem

US structural design relies on three primary codes that cover loads, concrete, and steel respectively. Each code is maintained by a separate professional organization and updated on a roughly 5-year cycle.

Regional Adoption

The International Building Code (IBC) is adopted by most US jurisdictions, either verbatim or with local amendments. IBC 2024 references ASCE 7-22 for loads, ACI 318-25 for concrete, and AISC 360-22 for steel — making those three codes the effective design standards in practice.

SI Note: All three codes publish dual-unit editions. ASCE 7 and ACI 318 use US Customary (lbf, ft, psi) as primary; SI (N, m, MPa) versions are available. AISC 360 is US Customary primary. This guide presents both unit systems for every formula.

2. LRFD Philosophy

All three codes use Load and Resistance Factor Design (LRFD), also called strength design. The fundamental inequality is:

LRFD Design Criterion — both unit systems
US & SIφRn ≥ ΣγᵢQᵢ
Where:
φ = strength reduction factor (material/failure-mode dependent)
Rn = nominal resistance (moment, shear, axial capacity)
γᵢ = load factor for load type i (dead, live, wind, seismic…)
Qᵢ = nominal load effect for load type i

Strength reduction factors φ are calibrated to achieve a target reliability index β ≈ 3.5, corresponding to a probability of failure on the order of 1 in 4,000 for individual members. Load factors amplify the nominal loads to account for variability in load magnitude.

LRFD vs ASD

AISC 360 also allows Allowable Strength Design (ASD), where the nominal resistance is divided by a safety factor Ω and compared to unfactored loads. LRFD is generally preferred for new design as it provides more consistent reliability across different load combinations.

A complete structural design follows a sequential flow. Each stage uses a specific standard or chapter of a standard.

Stage Details

  • Load Determination (ASCE 7-22): Establish dead, live, snow, wind, and seismic loads for the site and occupancy. Includes live load reduction for large tributary areas.
  • Load Combinations (ASCE 7 §2.3): Combine factored loads per the 7 LRFD combinations to find governing demands. ACI 318 §5.3 lists the same combinations adapted for concrete notation.
  • Structural Analysis: Determine force distribution (moment, shear, axial) using elastic or plastic analysis. AISC Chapter C covers stability analysis requirements for steel frames; ACI 318 Chapter 6 covers concrete frame analysis.
  • Member Design: Check flexure, shear, axial, deflection against code capacities. This guide series covers the US Standards approach for concrete (ACI 318) and steel (AISC 360).
  • Detailing: Reinforcement development lengths, connection design, seismic detailing requirements. ACI 318 Chapter 25 covers bar development; AISC Chapters D–J cover steel connections.

Key Differences from Eurocode

Engineers transitioning from Eurocode (EN 1992/1993/1998) should note:

  • Concrete strength: ACI uses cylinder strength f'c (28-day); EC2 uses cylinder fck. Values are directly comparable (f'c ≈ fck).
  • Rebar yield: US Grade 60 (fy = 420 MPa) ≈ EC2 B500 (fyk = 500 MPa) — different values.
  • Load combinations: ASCE 7 uses 7 LRFD combinations; EC uses 6 ULS combinations with partial factors.
  • Steel grades: A992 Fy = 345 MPa vs. S355 fy = 355 MPa — similar but not identical.
