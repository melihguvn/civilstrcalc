1. The Turkish Standards Ecosystem

Structural design in Turkey is governed by standards issued by Türk Standartları Enstitüsü (TSE) — the Turkish Standards Institution — alongside earthquake regulations published by the Ministry of Interior's Disaster and Emergency Management Presidency (AFAD). The legal framework requires compliance with both the material and structural design standards (TS series) and the mandatory earthquake code (TBDY).

Regulatory Framework

Turkey's building regulations derive their authority from the Yapı İşleri İnşaat Yönetmeliği (Building Works Construction Regulation), which mandates compliance with TSE standards. The TBDY (Türkiye Bina Deprem Yönetmeliği) is a statutory regulation, not merely a guideline — non-compliance is a legal matter. Buildings must satisfy both TS 500 for structural design adequacy and TBDY 2018 for seismic performance.

Key distinction: TS 500 covers all reinforced concrete design (gravity, lateral, seismic). TBDY 2018 provides additional and superseding requirements for earthquake-resistant design. Where TBDY conflicts with TS 500, TBDY governs.

TS EN Harmonized Standards

Since Turkey's EU accession negotiations and alignment with European technical norms, TSE has adopted many CEN (European Committee for Standardization) standards as TS EN standards. Engineers may use TS EN 1992 (Eurocode 2) for concrete design and TS EN 1993 (Eurocode 3) for steel design, provided the Turkish National Annexes are applied. In practice, TS 500 remains the dominant standard for RC design because of its deep integration with Turkish construction practice and TBDY 2018.

2. Design Philosophy — Limit State Design

TS 500:2000 uses Limit State Design (Sınır Hal Tasarımı), consistent with the Eurocode framework. Structural adequacy is checked at two levels:

  • Ultimate Limit State (ULS / Taşıma Gücü Sınır Hali): Ensures the structure does not collapse or lose its load-carrying capacity. Governs member sizing for flexure, shear, axial force, and combinations.
  • Serviceability Limit State (SLS / Kullanılabilirlik Sınır Hali): Ensures the structure remains serviceable — limits on deflection (L/250 for beams), crack width (≤ 0.3 mm for aggressive environments), and vibration.
TS 500 Design Criterion
TS 500Rd ≥ Ed
Where:
Rd = design resistance (reduced by material partial safety factors γc, γs)
Ed = design action effect (amplified by load factors from TBDY/TS 498 combinations)

Material Partial Safety Factors

Unlike ACI 318 which uses strength reduction factors φ applied to capacity, TS 500 follows the Eurocode convention of dividing material strength by partial safety factors:

MaterialCharacteristic StrengthPartial FactorDesign Strength
Concretefckγc = 1.5fcd = fck / 1.5
Reinforcementfykγs = 1.15fyd = fyk / 1.15
Structural steelfyγM0 = 1.00fyd = fy / 1.00

The concrete partial factor γc = 1.5 includes uncertainty in material production and in-situ casting. For prefabricated or factory-produced elements under quality control, TS 500 permits γc = 1.45. During seismic conditions, TBDY 2018 §5.4 uses the same factors but requires higher-ductility reinforcement grades.

3. TBDY 2018: A Landmark Update

The Türkiye Bina Deprem Yönetmeliği 2018 (Turkish Building Earthquake Code 2018) replaced the 2007 earthquake code and represents the most comprehensive overhaul of Turkish seismic design in two decades. It entered into force on 1 January 2019.

AFAD Spectral Map: TBDY 2018 uses probabilistic seismic hazard maps available at tdth.afad.gov.tr. For any site in Turkey, engineers can obtain SS (short-period) and S1 (1-second period) spectral accelerations at four return periods without manual table lookup.

Earthquake Design Class (DTS)

TBDY 2018 assigns every building an Earthquake Design Class (Deprem Tasarım Sınıfı, DTS) based on the DD-2 peak ground acceleration and the building's seismic design category. DTS 1 applies to the highest-hazard, most-important buildings; DTS 4 to lower-hazard ordinary structures. The DTS governs which ductility level is mandatory, what R factors may be used, and what detailing requirements apply.

4. Design Flow

A complete structural design for a building in Turkey follows this sequential process, each step governed by a specific standard:

Load Determination

Step 2

Step 3
Seismic Demand
TBDY Ch.3–4
Step 4
Structural Analysis
TBDY Ch.4 / TS 500
Step 5
Member Design
TS 500 / TBDY Ch.7
  • Load Determination (TS 498): Characteristic values of dead loads (self-weight), imposed loads by occupancy category, snow loads, and wind loads. TS 498 Table values govern gravity loads; TBDY Chapter 4 determines seismic mass.
  • Load Combinations (TBDY §4.4): TBDY 2018 specifies both gravity and seismic combinations. The critical ULS combinations include 1.4G, 1.2G + 1.6Q, 1.2G + Q ± E, and 0.9G ± E, where E is the design earthquake effect.
  • Seismic Demand (TBDY Ch.3–4): Determine design spectrum from AFAD maps, classify soil (ZA–ZF), compute SDS and SD1, select analysis method (ELF or modal), and calculate base shear Vt.
  • Member Design (TS 500 + TBDY Ch.7): Size beams for flexure and shear per TS 500 §8–9. For seismic systems, TBDY Chapter 7 imposes additional requirements — confinement zones, strong column–weak beam, capacity shear design.

Common mistake: Using Eurocode 2 (TS EN 1992) for RC design without also applying the Turkish National Annex can lead to incorrect partial factors and ductility requirements. Always use the TS EN + Turkish NA together, or default to TS 500.

Practical Observations

  • Concrete model: TS 500 uses a rectangular stress block of depth 0.8x (neutral axis depth), which differs slightly from ACI's β₁·c. For typical C25/30 concrete the difference in moment capacity is less than 2%.
  • Shear design: TS 500 uses a simplified diagonal tension approach (Vcr = 0.65·fctd·b·d) that is conservative for lightly reinforced members compared to ACI's detailed shear model but is well-calibrated for Turkish practice.
  • Seismic integration: Unlike ACI 318 where seismic detailing is an appendix, TBDY 2018 is a standalone regulation that references TS 500 for basic design and overrides it for seismic systems. This creates a two-layer design process.
