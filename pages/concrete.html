Compressive Strength↑ Top

Cube vs Cylinder Strength — The Critical Distinction

Concrete compressive strength is measured on standard specimens, but the world uses two different specimen shapes:

  • 150 mm cube (EN 1992, IS 456, most of Asia/Europe): The cube test gives a higher apparent strength because the loading platens restrain the ends of the specimen through friction. Reported as fck,cube.
  • 150×300 mm cylinder (ACI 318, Canada, South America): Less end restraint relative to height → lower measured strength. Reported as f'c.
Approximate conversion — normal-strength concrete
f'c (cylinder) ≈ 0.80 × fck,cube   [range 0.78–0.82]
EC2 notation: C25/30 means fck=25 MPa (cylinder) / fck,cube=30 MPa (cube)
ACI notation: f'c=25 MPa refers to the cylinder strength directly
Never mix cylinder and cube strengths in one calculation. EC2 C30/37 (fck=30 MPa cylinder) is NOT the same as ACI f'c=30 MPa — the latter is equivalent to ≈37.5 MPa in cube terms.

Secant Modulus Ec — Three Code Formulas

All three codes derive Ec empirically from compressive strength. The formulas differ because they were calibrated against different datasets and specimen conventions.

ACI 318-25 §19.2.2 — Normal-weight concrete (wc = 2,300 kg/m³)
Ec = 4,700√f'c  (MPa, cylinder strength)
Example: f'c=28 MPa → Ec=4,700×√28=24,870 MPa
EN 1992-1-1 §3.1.3 — Secant modulus at σ=0.4fcm
Ecm = 22·(fcm/10)0.3  (GPa, fcm in MPa)
Example: C25/30 → fcm=33 MPa → Ecm=22×(3.3)0.3=32.5 GPa
IS 456:2000 §6.2.3.1 — Short-term static modulus
Ec = 5,000√fck  (MPa, cube characteristic strength)
Example: M25 → fck=25 MPa → Ec=5,000×5=25,000 MPa

EC2 consistently gives the highest Ec values because fcm (mean strength) is ≥8 MPa above fck. ACI gives lower values partly because the cylinder strength is ~20% lower than the equivalent cube strength.

Long-Term Effective Modulus — Creep

Under sustained load, concrete creeps — strains continue to grow beyond the initial elastic deformation. The long-term effective modulus is:

Ec,eff = Ec / (1 + φ)
φ = creep coefficient (typically 1.5–3.0; depends on age at loading, ambient humidity, member thickness)
Typical interior column at 70% RH: φ ≈ 2.0 → Ec,eff ≈ Ec/3

Why Concrete Tensile Strength Matters Despite Being "Ignored"

In RC flexural design the tensile zone is cracked and the steel carries the tension. However, concrete tensile strength governs three critical phenomena:

  • Shear capacity Vc: ACI Vc=0.66λρ1/3√f'c·bwd — the √f'c term reflects diagonal tension cracking.
  • Crack control: The cracking moment Mcr=fr·Ig/yt determines when cracks form; crack widths are limited by bar spacing.
  • Punching shear: All punching shear equations in ACI and EC2 are a function of √f'c or fctd — the failure mode is tensile splitting around the perimeter.

Comparing Ec and fr Across Three Codes — Same Structural Concrete

Given: A reinforced concrete beam uses C25/30 concrete per EC2, which corresponds approximately to ACI f'c=25 MPa and IS 456 M30 (all representing roughly the same compressive strength in their native test specimen). Compute the elastic modulus and tensile (flexural) strength for each code.

Step 1 — Identify strength parameters
EC2 C25/30: fck=25 MPa (cylinder), fcm=25+8=33 MPa
ACI f'c=25 MPa (cylinder)   [≈ 25/0.80=31 MPa cube — close to C25/30 cube of 30 MPa]
IS 456 M30: fck=30 MPa (cube)   [cylinder equivalent ≈ 0.80×30=24 MPa ≈ f'c=25 MPa]
Step 2 — Elastic Modulus
ACI: Ec = 4,700√25 = 4,700×5 = 23,500 MPa
EC2: Ecm = 22×(33/10)0.3 = 22×(3.3)0.3 = 22×1.477 = 32,500 MPa
IS 456: Ec = 5,000√30 = 5,000×5.477 = 27,390 MPa
Step 3 — Tensile / Flexural Strength
ACI (modulus of rupture): fr = 0.62×1.0×√25 = 0.62×5 = 3.10 MPa
EC2 (mean tensile strength): fctm = 0.30×252/3 = 0.30×8.55 = 2.56 MPa
IS 456 (flexural tensile): fcr = 0.7√30 = 0.7×5.477 = 3.83 MPa
The IS 456 value is based on cube strength so it appears higher. The ACI modulus of rupture (flexural test) exceeds the EC2 mean tensile strength because ACI fr is a modulus of rupture from a beam bending test — higher than direct tension by the strain-gradient effect.
